Capture and follow-up — currently almost nothing

Around 98% of first-time visitors will not buy. Today the site has one way to keep any of them: an email popup that most people close.

What exists

  • Email newsletter popup (intrusive, fires too early)
  • A contact form on /pages/contact — 50 words of page copy around it
  • A consultation form on /pages/get-free-consultation with a WhatsApp button — the best lead page on the site, and it is not promoted anywhere

What is missing

  • Floating WhatsApp button on every page
  • Abandoned-cart recovery by WhatsApp and email
  • Post-purchase "how to take this" message on day 2
  • Review request on day 10
  • Refill reminder on day 25 — a 60-tablet pack at 2/day is exactly one month
  • Subscriptions on chronic-use products
  • Any reason to give an email other than "discounts"

Do this instead of the popup

  • Promote "Talk to our Vaidya on WhatsApp — free" as the primary capture offer. You already have the page; it just needs to be in the header and on every product page.
  • It converts far better than a discount popup for medicine, it produces a phone number rather than an email, and it starts a conversation you can close.
  • Keep the email popup, but at 20 seconds and once a month.

Claims — this is blocking your paid advertising

Not a legal opinion, but a commercial warning. 17 of the 111 pages carry disease or cure wording in the page title or meta description, including the homepage itself.

The homepage meta description names three conditions "Discover trusted Ayurvedic medicines for diabetes, kidney stones, digestion, and pain relief." That is the sentence Google prints under your name in search results, and it is the kind of claim that gets an ad account rejected.
WhereWording foundWhy it matters for B2C
Homepage meta description"diabetes, kidney stones"Diabetes, sexual function, fertility and stone "removal" are the hardest categories to advertise in India. Meta and Google will reject these ads, so paid traffic cannot be turned on until the wording changes — quite apart from exposure under the Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act and ASCI's Ayurvedic code.
Product titles"kidney stone removal", "hypertension", "blood sugar management", "male fertility and sperm count", "men's stamina", "leucorrhea"
Product URLsThe claims are in the slugs themselves, e.g. crushtone-tablets-kidney-stone-removal
Collection pages/collections/shop and /collections/tablet both name diabetes
Blog"the ultimate guide to permanent comfort", "natural treatment of kidney stones", "best treatment for leucorrhea"
Diabezeel page body"management of Type II Diabetes and newly detected NIDDM" (also spelled "Diabetese")
What to do — and the trap to avoid Get a lawyer to approve a phrase bank ("supports healthy blood sugar", "traditionally used for urinary comfort", "consult your physician") and rewrite titles, meta descriptions and blog headlines to it. Do not change the URLs without 301 redirects — those slugs are earning whatever rankings you currently have, and changing 47 product URLs carelessly would cost more traffic than the rewording saves. Change the visible title and meta first; migrate slugs later, with redirects, one batch at a time.

The stockist page is working against you

/pages/stockist — 479 words, no H1, no meta description, 44 sub-headings.

What it actually contains

  • A "Stockist Search" heading and a list of roughly 30 existing distributors
  • For each: company name, full address and a mobile number
  • Mostly Gujarat, some Rajasthan and Haryana
  • One entry reads "Address Not Available"
  • No form. No application process. No way to become a stockist.

Why this is a problem

  • It is a competitor's shopping list. A rival Ayurvedic brand's field manager can lift your entire Gujarat distribution network — names, towns, phone numbers — in one minute, and start calling them tomorrow.
  • It publishes partners' contact details, which they very likely did not expect to be indexed by Google.
  • The page occupies the exact URL a prospective distributor would click, and then offers him nothing to do.
  • It has no H1 and no meta description, so it does not even rank for the searches it might win.
What to do instead — split it into two pages 1. A public "Where to buy" page for consumers: city and pincode search showing shop name and area only, no phone numbers, no full addresses — enough for a patient to find stock, not enough for a competitor to poach. 2. A private partner list behind login for your field team and approved partners. 3. Point /pages/stockist itself at a new "Become a stockist" page with the application form. That single change converts your biggest B2B liability into your main B2B lead source.

The enquiry form — what is wrong with the one you have

/pages/contact has one form: Full Name, Phone Number, Email Address, Your Message, "Submit Message". The page itself is 50 words.

Problems

  • No enquiry-type selector, so a distributor enquiry lands in the same inbox as "where is my order"
  • No qualifying fields — you cannot tell a serious distributor from a curious consumer
  • No WhatsApp option, which is how this buyer actually prefers to talk
  • No response-time promise
  • No confirmation of what happens next
  • No map, so a buyer who wants to visit the plant cannot
  • Submissions are almost certainly not tracked as conversions — there is no GA4 on the site at all

The six-field B2B form that works

  • Name · Firm name · City / district
  • Drug licence number — the single best qualifier; time-wasters do not have one
  • GST number
  • Phone (WhatsApp) · What are you interested in: distribution / PCD / clinic supply / bulk
  • Keep it to six fields. Every extra field costs roughly 10% of submissions.
  • On submit: a WhatsApp deep link, a downloadable catalogue, and "we reply within one working day".
  • Route to a Google Sheet or free HubSpot CRM, and mark it as a GA4 conversion.

GEO — being cited by ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews

Generative engines do not rank pages; they assemble answers from sources they consider consistent and verifiable, and cite a handful. Being citable is a different job from ranking, and for a brand like yours it is winnable — because consistency and institutional provenance matter more than domain authority.
