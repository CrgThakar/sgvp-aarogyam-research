SGVP Aarogyam online — the store, the feed and the ad money

The growth plan treats the website as one of six channels and spends most of its pages on doctors, stockists and distributors. This page does the opposite. It covers only what can be run from a laptop — the store, the content, the social accounts and the campaign calendar — what that part alone can be expected to deliver, and in what order to do it.

What digital alone can be expected to deliver

Digital will not move 4,700 units a day. In Indian Ayurvedic pharma it carries 10–20% of volume. But it is the 10–20% with the best margin, it is the only channel that gives you the customer's phone number, and it is the first thing every doctor, distributor and sweet-shop owner checks before agreeing to meet you. So it has two jobs, and both are counted below.

Job 1 — sell directly

Own site + Amazon + quick-commerce. Measured in orders per day, AOV and repeat rate. This is the number in the table below.

Job 2 — make the other five channels close faster

A doctor who searches your name finds a real brand; a distributor finds a margin page and a WhatsApp number; a pharmacist finds reviews. Measured in partner enquiries per month and in how many field visits end in a first order. Free to build, and worth more than the direct sales in year one.

Part 1 — The revenue path: seven screens, not 110 pages

The audit lists what is broken. This lists what earns. A buyer passes through seven screens between seeing you and paying; money leaks at each one. Fix them in this order — the later screens are cheaper to fix and leak more.

1 · The ad or the search resultImpression
Leak: the listing looks like a generic Ayurvedic seller. No rating stars, no price, no "Made by SGVP".
Do: Merchant Center free listings + product review feed so stars show in Shopping. Title format Product — what it supports | SGVP Aarogyam. Sitelinks to /pages/quality and the doctor page.
2 · The landingFirst 3 seconds
Leak: the newsletter popup covers the hero, a 13-slide carousel with typos loads, and the certificate logos are broken images. This is the single largest loss in the funnel and it is free to fix.
Do: popup to 20 s / exit intent, once per 30 days. Slider 13 → 4 slides, typos fixed. Re-upload GMP / FDCA / FSSAI images linked to real PDFs. Never land an ad on the homepage — land on the product or the condition collection.
3 · The product pageWhere the decision happens
Leak: 43 of 47 products have under 120 words and 1–2 images. A first-time buyer of an Ayurvedic medicine will not spend ₹250 on that.
Do: the anatomy below. Start with the 8 hero SKUs only — a finished page on 8 products beats a thin page on 47.
4 · Add to cartBasket size
Leak: one ₹210 tablet pack, shipping eats the margin, no reason to add a second item.
Do: free shipping over ₹599 (shown as a progress bar in the cart), a 2+1 pack option on the PDP, a "goes with this" block (e.g. digestion tablet + triphala), and a 3-month course pack priced 12% below 3×.
5 · CheckoutBiggest silent loss
Leak: Indian D2C checkouts abandon at 60–75%. COD orders return at 15–30% and each return costs shipping both ways plus a broken seal.
Do: Razorpay with UPI first; 5% prepaid discount shown as a line item; COD kept but capped (no COD above ₹2,000, no COD on first order to a new pincode after two RTOs); abandoned-checkout WhatsApp at 30 min and email at 24 h.
6 · After the orderWhere trust is made
Leak: silence until the parcel arrives, then nothing. No review, no second order.
Do: Shiprocket tracking on WhatsApp, a "how to take this" message on day 2 (dosage, timing, what to avoid), review request on day 10, refill reminder on day 25.
7 · The second orderAll the profit
Leak: chronic-use products (Diabezeel, SG-Liv, Cardpro) are bought once when they should be bought twelve times.
Do: Shopify Subscriptions on every chronic SKU at 8% off with free shipping; refill broadcast at day 25; a "your course" page showing what they bought and when to reorder.

Hero product page anatomy

Above the fold

  • H1 = product name + what it supports (lawyer-approved wording only)
  • 5–7 images: front, back label with the ingredients panel, pack size in hand, one lifestyle, one lab/COA shot
  • Price, pack options (60s / 3-month course / 500s hospital), subscription toggle
  • Trust strip: GMP · AYUSH licence no. · Made at SGVP Aarogyam, Ahmedabad · COD · 7-day return
  • Star rating from real reviews — add reviews before spending on ads

Below the fold

  • 150–250 words: who it is for, how to take it, how long before it is judged
  • Composition table: each herb, quantity per tablet/5 ml, classical action, reference text
  • Contraindications, "consult a physician", pregnancy/child note
  • HSN code, GST rate, shelf life, batch COA download — this also satisfies the B2B buyer
  • 8–10 FAQs with FAQ schema — wins "People also ask" and is what AI answer engines quote
  • Medical disclaimer block on the template, not per product
One job, three results The composition table, COA and FAQ block are written once and then serve the retail buyer (conversion), Google (thin-content fix), and the doctor or distributor evaluating you (Job 2). Budget two weeks of one person's time for the 8 hero SKUs and treat the remaining 39 as a month-4 task.

The review engine — do this before spending on ads

Past buyers

Export every customer from Shopify. One WhatsApp and one email asking for a review, with the direct link. Target 10 reviews per hero SKU in two weeks.

The SGVP community

Gurukul alumni, satsang attendees, staff families. A genuine ask, never an incentivised one — Amazon removes incentivised reviews and can suspend the account.

The field team

~70 people on the ground. Three review requests per visit, via a QR code the retailer shows the customer. That is thousands of asks a month that no online-only competitor can match.

Part 4 — Social that feeds the funnel

Social will not move 100 units a day on its own. It does two things that everything else depends on: it makes the doctor, distributor or pharmacist who just heard your name trust you within 30 seconds of looking you up, and it supplies the creative that paid campaigns run on. Every channel in the growth plan converts better when the profiles look alive.

Read this before posting anything Ayurvedic medicines fall under the Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act and ASCI's Ayurvedic code. Do not name Schedule J conditions — diabetes, sexual function, fertility, obesity, kidney-stone "removal", height increase — as things a product treats or cures. No before/after images, no "guaranteed", no "permanent". Meta and Google reject these ads too, so this is a commercial constraint as much as a legal one. Have a lawyer approve a phrase bank ("supports healthy digestion", "traditionally used for joint comfort", "consult your physician") and let no one post outside it. Every post carries: Not a substitute for medical advice. Consult a qualified physician.

One shoot day → a month of content

  • Morning: 8 hero SKUs, studio-style — front, back label, in hand, pack opened, dosage. Feeds the website, Amazon A+, WhatsApp catalogue and ads all at once.
  • Midday: plant B-roll — press, blending, QC lab, packing line, warehouse.
  • Afternoon: the BAMS doctor records 8–12 answers in one sitting, one take each.
  • Output: ~30 posts and 12 reels. Edited in CapCut and Canva by an in-house person, not an agency.
  • Repeat once a month. Never run out of footage; never pay per asset.

Turn the field team into the content engine

  • A WhatsApp group where the ~70 field people drop 15-second clips: a retailer's shelf, a dispensary, a health camp, a happy customer.

Rules from day one

  • Conversions imported from GA4: purchase, WhatsApp click, distributor form, doctor form. Not page views.
  • Target cost per purchase ≤ ₹400 by month 2; expect ₹600–800 in week 1 and don't panic.
  • Negatives: recipe, how to make, patanjali, dabur, free, job, salary.
  • Move Shopping → Performance Max only after 50 recorded conversions.
  • Every ad lands on a page with reviews, the composition table and a WhatsApp button. Never the homepage.
  • Build ads around the herb, the routine and the institution — Meta's health policy blocks condition targeting and claims.

Who runs it

  • One in-house e-commerce person, 3–4 hours a week inside Google Ads and Meta Ads.
  • An AI assistant for keyword lists, 15 headlines at a time, Gujarati/Hindi ad variants, and reading exported CSVs. It cannot log in, set bids or create campaigns.
  • Google Smart Bidding handles bids; a human supplies what it cannot know — margin, stock, which city just reordered.
  • No agency until spend crosses ₹2 L/month. Below that, fees eat the budget.

Subscriptions

Shopify Subscriptions on every chronic SKU — Diabezeel, SG-Liv, Cardpro and the joint/digestion range. 8% off plus free shipping. For the category leaders this is 30–40% of D2C revenue, and it converts a ₹250 customer into a ₹3,000 one.

The list is the asset

WhatsApp Business app until 2,000 contacts, then the API via Interakt or Wati. Tag every contact: retail / doctor / retailer / distributor / lapsed. Never broadcast the same message to all four.

Offline feeds it

A QR on every pack and every retailer standee → a landing page with the batch COA, a WhatsApp button and "buy again". Every offline sale becomes an online customer you can reach for free, forever.

Part 7 — Measurement and the weekly rhythm

Eight numbers, one dashboard, one 45-minute meeting. Anything more gets ignored by week three.

Events to mark as conversions in GA4

  • purchase — with value and item, from the Shopify channel
  • whatsapp_click — the most common "conversion" on an Indian health site
  • distributor_form · doctor_form · bulk_form
  • coa_download — a strong B2B intent signal
  • subscribe_toggle — tells you which SKUs to push subscriptions on
  • UTM or a source parameter per field-team referral code

The one dashboard

  • Orders/day and revenue, split own site vs marketplace
  • Sessions by source, and conversion rate by source
  • AOV and returning-customer rate
  • Cost per purchase and blended ROAS
  • Reviews collected this week
  • Partner enquiries this week (Job 2)
  • RTO rate on COD orders
  • Stock cover in days on the 8 hero SKUs
Monday, 45 minutes, four questions 1. What did last week's numbers do, against the eight above?  2. Which campaign is furthest from its cost-per-purchase target, and what is the one change?  3. What are we shooting and publishing this week?  4. What is out of stock or about to be? — Ads off on anything that cannot ship. Nothing else on the agenda.

Guardrails — never break these

  • No claim outside the lawyer-approved phrase bank, on any page, post or ad.
  • No before/after images, no "cure", "permanent", "control", "guaranteed".
  • Same MRP online and offline, every channel, every month.
  • No advertising on a SKU with under two weeks of stock cover.
  • No incentivised reviews — it can cost the Amazon account.
  • Nothing goes live without a named medical reviewer on health content.

Where this plan stops

  • Distributors, stockists, PCD franchise, doctor detailing, sweet-shop and hotel B2B — see the growth plan.
  • Headless Astro / Next.js and the partner portal — a Stage 2 decision, not a month-1 one. Nothing in this plan is blocked by the current Shopify theme.
  • The new own-brand project, including A2 ghee — see the own-brand project plan. Keep the two brands' social accounts, ad accounts and analytics separate from day one.
