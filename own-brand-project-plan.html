One brand, two engines — A2 ghee first, then the Ayurvedic range

The research is done: the business model, the ghee market, the brand-name rounds and the architecture all sit in the research page. This is the plan that turns it into a company — what gets built in what order, what it costs, who owns it, and what has to be decided before the first rupee is spent. The ghee launch detail lives in its own page; this one is the project around it.

The shape of it, in one page

A house brand in the Himalaya / Patanjali mould: a master name that describes no product, so it can carry ghee today and roughly thirty Ayurvedic SKUs later. Everything is made by SGVP Aarogyam under third-party manufacturing; the pack carries "from SGVP Aarogyam, Ahmedabad" as an endorser line, which is both legally required and an asset in Gujarat.

Why ghee first, and why it is not the business

What ghee does for the project

  • It is legal to advertise. A food product can be marketed, photographed and boosted on Meta. An Ayurvedic medicine largely cannot — the Drugs & Magic Remedies Act blocks almost every hook you would want to use.
  • It builds the brand cheaply. The gaushala and Bilona video is the most-watched content in the category. Ghee earns the audience that the medicines will later sell to.
  • It opens the doors. Ghee is the base of ghritam, snehana and basti — the Ayurvedic clinics that buy ghee are the same buyers who will later stock the tablets.
  • Fast to market. FSSAI licence and a lab report, not a two-to-four-month proprietary-medicine approval.

Why it cannot be the whole business

  • Margin is thin outside D2C. Bilona ghee at B2B prices barely breaks even even on own milk — see the unit economics in the ghee plan.
  • Amul launched Desi Gir Cow A2 Bilona ghee at roughly ₹1,400/L on statewide distribution. You cannot win Gujarat on price.
  • One product means one purchase reason and no basket. A house with thirty SKUs has a customer who comes back for something else.
  • Ayurvedic tablets carry a far better contribution per unit and are what SGVP is actually equipped to make at scale.
The one-sentence strategy Use ghee to buy an audience, a retail shelf and a set of clinic relationships in Gujarat within twelve months — then put thirty Ayurvedic SKUs on top of an audience that already trusts the name, instead of launching them cold.

Brand architecture

[HOUSE NAME] word + device mark · Classes 5 · 3 · 29 · 30 · 32 · 35 from SGVP Aarogyam, Ahmedabad endorser line on every pack, front or back ├── Vatsalam — cow products line Class 29 / 30 │ Dohan A2 Gir Cow Ghee · honey · cold-pressed oils · gaushala range │ ├── [House] Ayurveda Class 5 · AYUSH licence via SGVP │ Hero SKUs — coined, separately trademarked (digestion, liver, │ joints, piles, women's health, immunity) │ Range SKUs — descriptive names, cheap to add │ └── [House] Care Class 3 · month 12+ toothpaste · hair oil · face pack · soap · baby care

Naming rule, from the Himalaya model: coined and trademarkable names for the hero medicines ("Liv.52", not "Liver Syrup"), descriptive names for the long tail. You can own a coined name; you cannot own a category word.

Relabelled vs own formulation

  • Relabelled (SGVP's existing formulas, your name on the pack): 4–6 weeks, no new approval, lowest risk. This is how waves 1 and 3 should start.
  • Own formulation: SGVP must get each proprietary medicine approved by the state licensing authority under Rule 158-B — adds 2–4 months per product.
  • Do own formulations only for the two or three products you intend to make the brand famous for, and only after wave 1 proves the brand can sell anything at all.

Pack and label — Rule 161, get it right once

  • Name, ingredients with quantities and the classical reference text, "Ayurvedic Proprietary Medicine"
  • Net content, batch, mfg/exp, licence number, manufacturer and marketer name and address, MRP, AYUSH warnings
  • No "cure / permanent / control" wording anywhere on pack or carton
  • A QR on every pack → batch COA, dosage, WhatsApp, reorder. This is what turns an offline sale into an online customer.
  • Ghee additionally: veg mark, nutrition panel, FSSAI number and logo, customer-care contact

The line most new brands miss: working capital Distributors take 21–30 days of credit. Ghee ties up raw material for about 45 days. Retailers reorder on 15-day terms. Add stock in transit and at the depot, and you need roughly 90 days of cost of goods funded and sitting still on top of the capital above — on the order of ₹8–12 L once the ghee and the first six medicines are both running. A brand that spends its last rupee on the launch batch cannot fund the reorder it just earned. Ring-fence this before wave 1 ships.

The SGVP relationship — the biggest asset and the biggest risk

SGVP holds the licence, the premises, the QC, the gaushala, the institutional reputation and roughly seventy field people. Every one of those is borrowed, not owned. The agreement is therefore the most important document in the project, and it should be written while everyone is enthusiastic rather than when something has gone wrong.

Clauses the manufacturing agreement must carry

  • Brand ownership — the mark, artwork and all creative are yours, unconditionally and on termination
  • Exclusivity — SGVP will not supply the same relabelled formulation to another marketer in your territory
  • Formula confidentiality for anything developed for you
  • Quality specs + a batch COA with every consignment; retained samples; a 12-month stability study
  • MOQ, price at 1k / 5k / 10k, and a price-revision notice period — so a cost increase cannot land mid-campaign
  • Lead time commitment and a remedy if it slips; 30–45 days from artwork approval is the normal expectation
  • Product-complaint and recall liability, clearly allocated
  • Exit — notice period, transfer of artwork and stability data, right to move to another factory

Ask explicitly, in the first meeting

  • Which licences are held — is there GMP under Schedule T? Copies will be needed for Amazon, 1mg and every distributor.
  • Do they already manufacture for other brands, and for whom?
  • Is there a co-branding fee for "Manufactured by SGVP Aarogyam" on the pack?
  • Will they issue the premises consent letter later, for a loan licence?
  • Who owns stability data and the shelf-life declaration?
  • The field team. Can the ~70 people carry your brand, on what commission, and is that written down? Assume the answer is no until it is in the agreement.
Channel conflict — settle it before the first pack is printed SGVP Aarogyam sells 47 SKUs of its own under its own name, to the same doctors, the same pharmacies and, eventually, the same distributors. Your brand will sit on that shelf next to theirs, made in the same building. Agree the separation in writing and in the positioning: SGVP's own range stays institutional and prescription-led, sold through doctors and stockists; the new brand is consumer-led, sold through retail, online and modern trade, at different pack sizes and a different price band. Overlapping formulations in the same territory at different prices is the fastest way to damage both brands and the relationship at once.

The rule that holds it together One price list everywhere. Same MRP online and offline, same trade margin for distributors and for marketplaces. If a retailer sees Blinkit cheaper than his landing cost, he stops stocking you — and in year two the retailer network will be worth more than the marketplace order.

The digital build — deliberately small

A 5–10 SKU brand does not need the apparatus of a 47-SKU one. It needs one store, one story told well, and proof. Everything here should be live before the first bottle ships, because a distributor or a clinic will look you up the same evening they meet you.

The stack

  • One Shopify store, Dawn or Sense theme, six apps maximum. No headless build — there is nothing to be gained from it at this size.
  • Razorpay with UPI first, COD capped, Shiprocket, GST invoicing
  • WhatsApp Business with a catalogue from day one; the API later, past 2,000 contacts
  • GA4, Search Console, Merchant Center, Meta Pixel + Conversions API — all connected before launch, not after
  • Judge.me or similar for reviews, on the product page from the first order
  • Separate ad accounts, analytics and social handles from SGVP Aarogyam's. Do not share pixels between two brands.

The five pages that do all the work

  • The product page — the gaushala film, the batch lab report as a download, three sizes, subscription toggle, FAQ, reviews, WhatsApp button
  • The story page — SGVP, the Gurukul, the gaushala, the people. This is what a gifting buyer and a distributor read.
  • The proof page — FSSAI, AYUSH licence, GMP, every batch COA, photos of the plant. Linked from every product.
  • The trade page — margins, MOQ, territory, a six-field enquiry form, WhatsApp. One page, and it will pay for the site.
  • The gifting page — box options, MOQ 100, lead time, a form that goes to WhatsApp. Live by mid-October or it is a 2027 page.
The one piece of content that matters most A single well-made 60-second film of the Bilona process — curd setting, the wooden churner, the makkhan, the slow cooking, the cows it came from — is the most-watched content in this category and outperforms a year of advertising. Shoot it properly, once, in phase 1. Everything else can be a phone.

Detailed campaign structure, budgets, content pillars and the social calendar are in the SGVP digital growth plan — the mechanics transfer directly; only the phrase bank changes, because a food brand may say things an Ayurvedic medicine may not.

The next 30 days

Nothing here needs capital beyond about ₹1.5 L, and everything below unblocks something downstream. The order matters.

A note on Diwali 2026 Diwali falls on 8 November 2026 and corporate gifting orders close by the end of October. From a standing start today, catching it means a house name this week, packaging at the printer within three weeks, and product in hand by mid-October. That is possible but tight, and it only works if the gifting SKU is the priority. If the name slips past mid-October, plan the launch properly for winter — chyawanprash and immunity season runs November to January and is the second-best window of the year — rather than shipping a compromised first impression into the busiest gifting fortnight in the market.
